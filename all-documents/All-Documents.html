How Team Suresh Fitness collects, uses, stores, and protects personal information across its website and web-based membership platform.

Full page: privacy.html

This Privacy Policy applies to the TSF Digital Platform operated by Team Suresh Fitness (“TSF”, “we”, “us”, or “our”), based in Caranzalem, Goa, India. 2. Information we collect

2.1 Public website and payment page

  • Aggregate page-visit analytics only if an analytics service is enabled.
  • The public website does not intentionally collect information through its main pages unless a feature expressly says otherwise.
  • The macro calculator operates in the visitor’s browser. Information entered into it is not sent to TSF servers or stored by TSF.
  • The payment page displays payment details in a UPI QR or link. The page itself does not store payment amounts or personal information on TSF servers. Payment records maintained in the membership portal are described below.

2.2 Identity and contact information

  • Full name and Google account email address for account identification and login.
  • Optional Google profile photo.
  • WhatsApp or mobile number for service messages and fee reminders.
  • Emergency contact name and number for training-related emergencies.

2.3 Membership and fitness information

  • Selected program and preferred joining date.
  • Optional gender and date of birth, where collected for class grouping or age-appropriate programme allocation.
  • Height, weight, fitness goals, and optional medical notes or injury details, to the extent requested for programme guidance and training safety.
  • Attendance history, session count, programme days remaining, and coach notes.

2.4 Fee and payment records

  • Payment amount and date, payment status, payment reference or Razorpay payment ID, and receipt URL.
  • Details needed to reconcile fees, identify overdue amounts, and provide payment history.
  • Card details and UPI credentials are handled by the relevant payment service and are not intended to be stored on TSF servers.

2.5 Administrative, event, and technical information

  • Admin credentials and role information, changes made to member records, audit logs, internal admin notes, and member follow-up flags.
  • Event-interest records linking a member, an event, and the time interest was recorded.
  • Security-related technical records such as HTTP requests and IP addresses may be processed by hosting or security providers.

Data minimisation. Height, weight, medical notes, and other fitness information should only be collected when needed for a clear membership, coaching, or safety purpose. Avoid entering detailed medical histories into free-text notes unless the studio has approved a clear need and access rule.

3. How we use information

  • Membership management: identify members, manage enrolment and schedules, show profiles, and track attendance.
  • Fees and payments: track payments and outstanding dues, calculate due dates, send fee reminders, reconcile records, and provide receipts.
  • Member communications: send service messages such as welcome emails, payment receipts, fee reminders, event notifications, and programme milestones.
  • Coaching and safety: provide relevant fitness guidance, help coaches understand safety-relevant restrictions, and contact the emergency contact when appropriate.
  • Events: record interest and send information about the events a member has expressed interest in.
  • Administration: prepare internal reports about revenue, membership growth, attendance, programme use, and outstanding fees, and investigate support requests or suspected misuse.
  • Security: authenticate users, restrict access, keep audit records, and protect the platform against abuse.

We do not sell or rent member personal information. We do not share member information with unrelated gyms or businesses for their own marketing. We do not publicly use member photographs or personal details without appropriate permission. Promotional communications should only be sent where the member has opted in.

Data Breach Response Plan

A step-by-step response for suspected loss, exposure, unauthorised access, or misuse of member personal information.

Full page: data-breach-response.html

1. When to activate this plan

Activate this plan if there is suspected or confirmed unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration, loss, or unavailability of member data. Examples include an exposed database, compromised administrator account, a lost device containing exported member records, a misdirected spreadsheet, a stolen session token, or an email or WhatsApp message sent to the wrong recipient.

2. Immediate actions: contain and preserve

  1. Report immediately. Staff and coaches must contact the studio owner or incident lead as soon as a concern is noticed. Do not wait to prove the breach.
  2. Contain. Revoke suspect sessions or tokens, disable compromised accounts, rotate affected secrets, restrict exposed storage, and pause vulnerable integrations where needed.
  3. Preserve evidence. Record detection time, reporter, affected system, relevant logs, and actions taken. Preserve copies securely; do not post screenshots or member data in public chats.
  4. Protect people. If the incident exposes health notes, emergency contacts, or personal phone numbers, restrict further access immediately and assess direct safety risks.
  5. Use a trusted channel. Avoid sending sensitive incident details through a possibly compromised account.

Internal target: start containment within hours of detection where technically possible. Do not delay containment while waiting for a full investigation.

3. Assess and document

The incident lead must establish, to the extent reasonably possible:

  • What happened, when it began, when it was detected, and how it was stopped.
  • Which systems and vendors were involved.
  • Types and approximate number of people and records affected.
  • Whether identity or contact information, payment references, emergency details, or health and fitness notes were involved.
  • Whether information was viewed, copied, changed, deleted, or made publicly accessible.
  • Likely harm and immediate safety, fraud, or misuse risks.
  • Containment actions, recovery steps, responsible people, and open questions.

Separate confirmed facts from assumptions. Do not delete logs that may help determine scope.

4. Notification assessment

Legal timing must be confirmed at the time of an incident. The official DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified with phased commencement. Rule 7 contains breach-notification requirements, including notice to affected people without delay and an initial notification to the Board without delay, followed by further details within 72 hours unless the Board allows more time. Rule 7 falls within the group scheduled for commencement 18 months after Gazette publication, i.e. 13 May 2027. Counsel must confirm the provisions actually in force and any other applicable duties on the incident date.

Do not assume that 72 hours is always the only legal deadline or that it applies in the same way before a provision takes effect. The incident lead should consult legal counsel promptly and document the decision.

Operational target from the source requirements: prepare to notify affected members by email and WhatsApp within 72 hours when appropriate, while separately meeting any applicable statutory timeline. Do not delay a legally required notification until this internal target.

5. Member and authority communications

If notification is warranted, use clear language and include the nature of the incident, the types of information involved where known, likely consequences, actions taken, practical steps members can take, and a contact for questions. Do not speculate or include another member’s information.

We are contacting you about a security incident affecting the TSF Digital Platform. We detected it on [date and time] and took steps to contain it. The information currently identified as affected is [categories]. The incident may involve [known or reasonably anticipated consequences]. We recommend [practical steps]. We are investigating and will provide material updates. Contact [privacy or support contact] with questions.

The incident lead approves communications with legal counsel where available. Keep copies of notifications and a record of who was notified, when, by which channel, and why.

6. Recovery and remediation

  1. Fix the cause and verify that exposed access is closed.
  2. Restore from a known-good backup only after checking that restoration will not reopen the vulnerability or undo valid deletion requests.
  3. Reset credentials or rotate secrets where required.
  4. Review access permissions, row-level security policies, audit logs, vendor settings, and data exports.
  5. Confirm critical functions work and that no unexpected access remains.
  6. Document decisions, technical fixes, communications, and residual risks.

7. Post-incident review

Within a reasonable period after containment, the owner leads a review of root cause, impact, response effectiveness, vendor involvement, process gaps, and preventive measures. Assign an owner and due date to every corrective action. Update staff guidance and this plan where needed.

Admin or owner Member, payment, event, and operational data needed to run TSF. Use a unique admin account; avoid shared logins and unnecessary exports.
Developer or support Temporary minimum access needed for a specific support task, authorised by the owner. Access must be time-limited, logged where possible, and revoked after the task.

Permissions must be enforced in the application and database. Test database row-level security separately; hiding buttons in the interface is not sufficient.

3. Authentication and credentials

  • Use Google OAuth and Supabase Auth as configured; never store Google passwords in TSF systems.
  • Use unique accounts and a strong, unique password for any service that uses a password.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication for provider and admin accounts where available.
  • Store API keys and secrets only in approved secret-management or deployment settings, never in public repositories, client-side code, or screenshots.
  • Revoke sessions and rotate credentials immediately if compromise is suspected.
  • Remove access immediately when a staff member or contractor leaves or no longer needs it.

4. Devices and remote work

  • Use supported, updated devices with screen lock and device encryption where available.
  • Do not leave admin sessions open on shared computers. Sign out when finished.
  • Do not download full member lists to personal devices unless authorised and necessary.
  • Keep devices containing exports physically secure. Report lost or stolen devices promptly.
  • Do not use unapproved public computers or unsecured networks for sensitive admin work.
  • Delete temporary exports and local files when the approved task ends.

5. Storage, sharing, and communication

  • Use the approved database, file storage, and business communication channels.
  • Before sending member information, verify the recipient and share only the minimum necessary.
  • Use recipient checks and restricted access when sharing reports. Do not place open links to member files in public repositories or websites.
  • Do not put health notes, payment details, or access tokens in public source code, issue trackers, or general chat channels.
  • Do not send passwords, OTPs, or payment credentials over WhatsApp or email.
  • Do not use real member data in development or testing when synthetic or test data will work.

6. Technical safeguards

The current technical specification calls for HTTPS and TLS, encryption at rest, role-based access control, row-level security, audit logging, restricted sessions, daily backups with seven-day retention, and hosting-level DDoS protection. The system owner and developer must verify these controls in the deployed configuration and retain evidence of verification. Do not claim a control is active merely because a document says it should be.

Before each significant release, test that members cannot query other members’ records, coaches cannot view contact or payment data, and unauthorised users cannot access admin routes or private files.

7. Physical security

  • Keep printed member records and incident notes in a locked location.
  • Dispose of paper containing personal information using secure shredding or an equivalent process.
  • Do not leave attendance sheets or payment reports visible to visitors or other members.
  • Limit access to the studio’s admin devices and printed documents.

8. Retention, deletion, and incidents

Follow the Data Retention & Deletion Policy. Use the Data Breach Response Plan for suspected incidents. Staff must not independently erase relevant evidence, hide an incident, or promise a member that an incident has no impact before it has been assessed.

9. Training, enforcement, and review

Every person with access must read the Staff Onboarding & Data Handling Guide and confirm they understand the rules. Violations may result in access suspension, retraining, termination of access, or other appropriate action. The owner reviews this policy at least annually and after a material incident or platform change.

N · Member-facing summary

Your Data at TSF

A plain-language summary members can read inside the TSF member portal.

Full page: your-data.html

What information do we keep?

We keep the information needed to manage your membership and provide the services you use. Depending on the details you provide and the portal features enabled, this may include:

  • Your name, email address, optional profile photo, and WhatsApp or mobile number.
  • Your emergency contact details.
  • Your selected programme, joining date, and relevant membership information.
  • Optional fitness information such as height, weight, goals, and safety-relevant medical notes.
  • Your attendance and session information and coach notes.
  • Your payment amount, payment date, status, reference, and receipt information.
  • Your interest in studio events and records needed for security or support.

Why do we use it?

We use your information to manage membership, maintain attendance, track fees, send service notifications, provide receipts, support coaching, and help staff respond to safety concerns. We do not sell or rent your personal information or give it to unrelated businesses for their marketing.

Who can see it?

  • You: your own profile, membership, and payment information.
  • Your coach: information needed for your assigned programme, such as your name, programme, attendance, and relevant safety notes. Coaches should not see your payment history, phone number, email, or emergency contact information.
  • Authorised studio admin: information needed to operate memberships, payments, and the studio.
  • Service providers: limited information needed to host the portal, sign you in, process payments, or deliver email and WhatsApp messages.

How are fees and reminders handled?

The portal can display your fee status and send reminders. A reminder is not a payment receipt, and it does not automatically renew your membership. Your next membership period is updated after the studio confirms the payment and renewal.

How long do we keep it?

Our current planned retention periods include 12 months for inactive profile and contact data, 12 months for attendance history, 6 months for medical notes, 3 months for notification logs, 6 months after an event for event-interest information, and 8 years for payment and accounting records under the proposed schedule. The payment-record period is subject to confirmation of the applicable accounting or legal requirement. Some information may be retained longer where a valid legal obligation or dispute requires it.

What can you ask us to do?

You can ask to see your information, correct it, request deletion, or raise a privacy concern. Some payment records may need to be kept for accounting or legal reasons. We verify requests before changing or deleting data.
