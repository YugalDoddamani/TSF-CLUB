How it works

The maths behind the numbers

01

Basal Metabolic Rate

Your BMR is the energy your body burns at rest — breathing, circulation, cell repair. We calculate it with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the most accurate formula for the general population.

02

Total Daily Energy Expenditure

TDEE is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. A desk job gives 1.2x. Someone training six days a week uses 1.725x. This is your maintenance calorie level.

03

Goal adjustment

To lose fat, we subtract 500 calories (about 0.5 kg per week). To gain muscle, we add 300. The adjustment is deliberately conservative — aggressive cuts and bulks rarely stick.

04

Macro split

Protein is set at 2 g per kg of bodyweight — the standard for people who train. Fat is 25% of total calories. Carbs fill the rest. This split supports both performance and body composition.

Common questions

Before you track

Do I have to hit these numbers exactly?

No. Aim within 5–10% of each target. Consistency over weeks matters far more than precision on any single day. If you hit your protein and stay near your calorie target, you're doing it right.

Why is protein set so high?

Two grams per kilogram of bodyweight is the evidence-based range for people who train regularly. It preserves muscle during a cut, supports growth during a bulk, and keeps you fuller for longer. If you have kidney concerns, talk to a doctor before increasing protein.

Should I count macros or just calories?

Start with calories for the first two weeks. Once that feels natural, layer in macros. Most people find that hitting protein is the single biggest lever for body composition — the carb and fat split matters less.

How often should I recalculate?

Every 4–6 weeks, or whenever your weight changes by more than 2 kg. As you lose or gain weight, your maintenance calories shift. Recalculating keeps the targets honest.

Can I use this if I'm not training?

Yes. Set your activity level to Sedentary or Light. The protein target will be slightly higher than a non-trainer needs, but it won't hurt. If you're completely sedentary, you can drop protein to 1.6 g per kg.
